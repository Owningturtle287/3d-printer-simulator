## Approximation boundaries

- Scene dimensions use millimeters and the published chassis envelope. Internal geometry, labels, fasteners, glass, cables and nozzle hardware are estimated from photos and diagrams. There was no attached image set in this turn, manufacturer CAD or physical unit to measure. Studio materials and textures are procedural. This scene has not been validated for photorealistic parity.
- The physical scene models carriage XY and bed Z. Coordinate checks use the published printable areas. The mechanical tip spacing uses 20.5 mm as a visualization estimate. Homing, switch contact, temperature curves, probing, purge and park behavior are simulator models. Collision checks cover reachable coordinates, plate presence and basic manual part clearance. Detailed toolhead-versus-part collisions, kinematic calibration, acceleration blending and full polymer or airflow physics are outside this build.
- The embedded touchscreen uses an original interface with controls documented for the X2D. The firmware, screens, diagnostics and network stack are proprietary. The chamber-camera view is rendered from a camera position in the scene. Audio is synthesized from motion, extrusion and fan state.
- The default hardware routing has two feeder branches. The simulator does not implement Bambu Bus, multiple AMS units, the optional Filament Track Switch or device pairing. Compatibility and drying profiles are conservative defaults and do not certify support for every spool and firmware combination. Virtual RFID is inferred from the selected spool profile. Fault injection models interruptions and recovery through the state machine.
- Printer profiles, virtual AMS information, imported files, settings and history are browser-local. Reloading discards an in-progress print and the part currently on the plate. Background throttling can slow progression. A physical iPhone and native desktop installers are outside the verification performed here.

## Print file compatibility

G-code supports linear moves, XY arcs with I/J offsets, relative/absolute coordinates and extrusion, units, coordinate resets, tool selection, supported temperatures, fan, dwell, and user pauses. Deposition is generated from parsed extrusion moves. Machine-specific startup, end and recognizable X2D filament-change sections are handled by the simulator. Recognized Bambu tool hints choose default mappings. The printer draws only from the simulated AMS or external spool. Firmware macros and all slicer/profile combinations are not implemented. Unsupported geometry commands and out-of-bounds toolpaths surface an import or preparation error.

Supported sliced 3MF archives contain printable G-code entries and may contain multiple plates, previews or Bambu settings. The unsliced fallback supports small closed triangular meshes with standard transforms. It centers them and creates contours and alternating infill. It does not produce supports, modifiers, complex material assignments or firmware macros, and is intended for inspecting prints in the simulator. Maximum upload is 64 MB, executable plates are limited to 650,000 commands, and the lightweight mesh slicer accepts up to 300,000 triangles.

## 3DBenchy

The bundled toolpath and rendered thumbnail are derived from **#3DBenchy by CreativeTools**, designed by Daniel Norée. The upstream source is [CreativeTools/3DBenchy at b542785](https://github.com/CreativeTools/3DBenchy/tree/b542785f9642ab39db8a778a0ccc934b3ebd829a/Single-part) and the publisher lists [CC0 1.0](https://www.3dbenchy.com/license/) for redistribution. The generated job is 60 × 31 × 48 mm with 0.20 mm layers. It is an in-simulator print file. Regenerate it with `node tools/benchy.mjs /absolute/path/to/3DBenchy.stl`.
