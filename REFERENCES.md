## Interior and cooling references

The toolhead uses a centrifugal part cooling impeller in the removable cover and two separate side-mounted axial fans for the heatsinks. The left heater and right auxiliary airflow assemblies have separate drive state. The fan mapping follows the [X2D Bambu Studio profile and templates](https://github.com/bambulab/BambuStudio/tree/master/resources/profiles/BBL/machine) and [Bambu Lab X2D manual, sections 1.2 and 6.5](https://csm.bblcdn.com/hub/7c58718aaa2e40edab56efb87419a96a.pdf). The [front cover replacement listing](https://shop3duniverse.com/collections/bambu-lab-products/products/toolhead-front-cover-assembly-x2d), [X2D hotend cooling fan replacement guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gzuZGA7baPE) and [hotend cooling fan part photos](https://spool3d.ca/bambu-lab-cooling-fan-for-hotend-x2d/) supplement the supplied chamber photos.

The new owner photographs show the AMS 2 Pro rollers and funnels, rear connectors, X2D auxiliary extruder cover, paired buffer sliders, touchscreen pivot, left-mode shutter, purge scoop, recessed rear screw, and factory calibration board. The [X2D manual, sections 1, 2, and 6](https://csm.bblcdn.com/hub/7c58718aaa2e40edab56efb87419a96a.pdf) confirms the upper/left and lower/right routing, cold/hot airflow modes, and the right-side optical calibration target. Small hardware and cable dimensions are photo-based estimates. The calibration board travels with the bed and its markings approximate the photographed target. The mode marker and rear buffer sliders animate from the simulator's heater and filament-feed state.

## Approximation boundaries

- Scene dimensions use millimeters and the published chassis envelope. Internal geometry, labels, fasteners, glass, cables and nozzle hardware are estimated from photos and diagrams. The supplied close-ups of the open and assembled toolhead and chamber informed the heatsink, silicone sock, camera housing, feed routing, drag chain, rail profile and duct revisions. Studio materials and textures are procedural. There was no manufacturer CAD or physical unit to measure. This scene has not been validated for photorealistic parity.
- The physical scene models carriage XY and bed Z. Coordinate checks use the published printable areas. The mechanical tip spacing uses 20.5 mm as a visualization estimate. Homing, switch contact, temperature curves, probing, purge and park behavior are simulator models. The left nozzle stays fixed vertically during selection, the auxiliary hotend lowers and the bed position compensates for the selected print datum. Exact linkage travel, cable routing and clearances are reconstructed from imagery. Individual fan thresholds and rotor speeds are simulation values. Collision checks cover reachable coordinates, plate presence, manual clearance and travel below previously deposited material using a 1.5 mm height grid. Full toolhead-envelope collisions, kinematic calibration, acceleration blending and polymer or airflow physics are outside this build.
- The embedded touchscreen uses an original interface with controls documented for the X2D. The firmware, screens, diagnostics and network stack are proprietary. The chamber-camera view is rendered from a camera position in the scene. Audio is synthesized from motion, extrusion and fan state.
- The default hardware routing has two feeder branches. The simulator does not implement Bambu Bus, multiple AMS units, the optional Filament Track Switch or device pairing. Compatibility and drying profiles are conservative defaults and do not certify support for every spool and firmware combination. Virtual RFID is inferred from the selected spool profile. Fault injection models interruptions and recovery through the state machine.
- Printer profiles, virtual AMS information, imported files, settings and history are browser-local. Reloading discards an in-progress print and the part currently on the plate. Background throttling can slow progression. A physical iPhone and native desktop installers are outside the verification performed here.

## Print file compatibility

G-code supports linear moves, XY arcs with I/J offsets, relative/absolute coordinates and extrusion, units, coordinate resets, tool selection, supported temperatures, fan, dwell, and user pauses. Deposition is generated from parsed extrusion moves. Machine-specific startup, end and recognizable X2D filament-change sections are handled by the simulator. Recognized Bambu tool hints choose default mappings. The printer draws only from the simulated AMS or external spool. Firmware macros and all slicer/profile combinations are not implemented. Unsupported geometry commands and out-of-bounds toolpaths surface an import or preparation error.

Supported sliced 3MF archives contain printable G-code entries and may contain multiple plates, previews or Bambu settings. The unsliced fallback supports small closed triangular meshes with standard transforms. It centers them and creates contours, sparse alternating infill, and three layers of solid skin measured locally above and below each exposed surface. It does not produce supports, modifiers, complex material assignments or firmware macros, and is intended for inspecting prints in the simulator. Maximum upload is 64 MB, executable plates are limited to 650,000 commands, and the lightweight mesh slicer accepts up to 300,000 triangles.

## 3DBenchy

The bundled toolpath and rendered thumbnail are derived from **#3DBenchy by CreativeTools**, designed by Daniel Norée. The upstream source is [CreativeTools/3DBenchy at b542785](https://github.com/CreativeTools/3DBenchy/tree/b542785f9642ab39db8a778a0ccc934b3ebd829a/Single-part) and the publisher lists [CC0 1.0](https://www.3dbenchy.com/license/) for redistribution. The generated job is 60 × 31 × 48 mm with 0.20 mm layers. Local top and bottom skins close the exposed deck, cabin floor, cargo well, and roof; cross sections retain openings such as the chimney bore. It is an in-simulator print file. Regenerate it with `node tools/benchy.mjs /absolute/path/to/3DBenchy.stl`.
